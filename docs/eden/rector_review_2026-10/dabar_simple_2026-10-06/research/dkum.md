# DKUM comparator review for UIST EDEN

> Comparator research retained from policy preparation. UIST use/adaptation suggestions below are historical; the [accepted pack](../README.md) governs current UIST wording and responsibilities (rector acceptance reported 6 October 2026).

**Checked:** 6 October 2026 (Europe/Skopje)  
**Scope:** official Digital Library of the University of Maribor (DKUM) pages, one live DKUM item, University of Maribor’s Open Science Policy, the University Library’s research-data deposit guide, and the matching re3data record. DKUM is a Slovenian institutional repository owned by the University of Maribor (UM); its rules are comparator evidence, not UIST commitments.

## Policy field summary for a simplified UIST policy

| Area | DKUM evidence | UIST adaptation recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Ownership and governance | Live DKUM describes itself as UM’s institutional repository; re3data assigns general responsibility to the University of Maribor. UM’s policy assigns repository maintenance and data-stewardship support to UM, coordinated with the University of Maribor Library. | Name UIST and rectorate-appointed manager **Pavel Taskov** as accountable owner/manager. State the operational support contact and distinguish UIST decisions from any hosting or service-provider duties. |
| Shortness and structure | The DKUM policy is compact and uses five labelled areas: metadata, data, content, submission and preservation. Its text is mostly short paragraphs and bullet-like conditions. | Use the same five-part spine for a short UIST policy: metadata; accepted content and deposit; access/reuse; preservation/withdrawal; accessibility/privacy/contact. Keep detailed instructions in linked guidance. |
| Accepted content | DKUM’s live policy says “all types of materials,” then explicitly names submitted, accepted and published versions and records version/date, peer-review status and publication status. The introduction names publications, theses, books/materials and research data. re3data broadens this to dataset, image, text and other. | Define a small UIST content list: Macedonian/English publications, theses and dissertations where approved, educational materials, and research datasets/other research outputs. Retain version, date, review and publication-status fields. |
| Languages | DKUM policy principal languages: English, Slovenian, German, Croatian and Hungarian. re3data lists Slovene and English as research-data languages; the UI is Slovenian/English. | Set UIST’s principal publication languages to **Macedonian and English**. Require Macedonian or English discovery metadata where UIST decides it is needed; do not inherit DKUM’s five-language list. |
| Deposit eligibility and quality | Accredited members, academic staff, registered students and employees may deposit, and authors submit their own work. Eligible depositors must deposit metadata and full texts, with visibility delayed for publisher/funder embargoes. The administrator checks depositor eligibility, scope and layout/format; internal subject specialists check validity/authenticity. | Limit deposit to UIST authors/staff/students or authorised delegates; record depositor authority and affiliation. Require a rights/licence declaration and basic file/metadata checks. Keep embargo as a controlled status with reason and end date. |
| Metadata licence and fields | DKUM’s metadata reuse condition permits non-profit reuse with OAI identifier or original-record link and DKUM attribution; metadata access may be controlled. A live record exposes rich descriptive metadata, checksum, identifiers, usage counts and many citation exports. | Adopt **CC0 for UIST metadata** if rectorate approves; state a narrow personal-data exception. Require title, creators, abstract/keywords, language, type/status/version/date, rights, identifier, affiliation, checksum/fixity and citation metadata. Publish only interfaces UIST actually operates. |
| File/object licences | DKUM policy says full-item reuse follows original copyright and rights statements and item-specific conditions. A live thesis is CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. The re3data registry lists CC, ODC, Apache 2.0, OGL, CC0 and other licences for research data. | Keep metadata rights separate from file rights. Require each item/dataset to carry a rights/licence value; distinguish open, embargoed, restricted and metadata-only access. Do not make all uploaded files CC0. |
| Access and requests | Metadata and full-item access may be controlled. Full-item non-profit display/use is generally permitted with attribution, source link, rights statements and no alteration; commercial sale needs permission. Embargoes last until publisher/funder expiry; rights violations trigger removal. re3data records database access open and data access open/restricted. | Make public access the default where rights permit. Require a documented reason, approver and review date for embargo/restriction. If UIST offers copy requests, route them to Pavel Taskov or a delegated role and retain the decision record. |
| Preservation and withdrawal | DKUM retains items at least 20 years; backs up regularly; retains the original bitstream plus upgraded formats; has no functional-specification policy. Withdrawal is discouraged, with listed reasons (publisher rules, proven copyright/plagiarism, legal/security, falsified research); withdrawn items are deleted and their identifiers/URLs are not retained. Closure transfers the database to another appropriate archive. | Set a UIST retention target and continuity plan that rectorate can resource. State original-file retention, backups, fixity/version records, migration, export/closure and a withdrawal/tombstone rule. Decide explicitly whether identifiers remain resolvable after withdrawal; DKUM’s deletion rule is a caution because UIST may want persistent citation links. |
| Identifiers and interoperability | Live record PID is `20.500.12556/DKUM-99783` with Handle URL and COBISS.SI-ID. Registry lists HDL/other repository PIDs, ORCID/ResearcherID, OAI-PMH, Dublin Core, OpenAIRE Guidelines and repository-developed schemas; versioning and usage statistics are marked yes. | Choose only identifiers and endpoints UIST will maintain (for example an approved PID/Handle or DOI plan, author ORCID, Dublin Core/OpenAIRE-aligned metadata, OAI-PMH if deployed). Treat registry claims as a readiness reference until UIST tests each endpoint. |
| Accessibility and privacy | DKUM gives larger/smaller-font controls and publishes a cookie/consent page; its public English privacy material covers cookies and AAI/analytics rather than repository takedown or full processing rules. | Link UIST’s supplied privacy PDF as the governing privacy source and add only repository-specific fields, retention, public metadata and takedown/contact routing. State file-accessibility duties for deposited PDFs and datasets separately from the website’s accessibility statement. |
| Support | DKUM footer provides `dkum@um.si`; the UM Library guide provides `ukm@um.si` for research-data guidance. | Publish one UIST repository support address plus a separate privacy/rights route. Keep routine support, rights/takedown and privacy requests visibly distinct. |

## Registry versus live claims

The re3data record is a registry description with editing status 2026-08-26, not a contract or proof that every advertised capability is live. It reports 76,539 records, while the DKUM home page displayed 77,312 records when checked on 6 October 2026; the figures are snapshots, not a stable inventory. The live DKUM policy is the stronger source for deposit, metadata, access, rights and preservation wording. The live item page is evidence that DKUM currently exposes a PID, Handle URL, checksum, citation exports, usage counts and an item-level Creative Commons licence. The registry is the stronger source for classification, repository identifiers, API/standards fields and listed data-licence categories.

DKUM is a useful model for a short UIST policy because it separates metadata rules, object/data reuse, accepted-content scope, deposit checks and preservation/withdrawal. UIST should copy that separation and concise condition style while adapting the language rule to Macedonian/English, naming Pavel Taskov, linking UIST’s privacy PDF, and avoiding unapproved promises about external partners, indefinite preservation, software, PID services or an OAI-PMH endpoint.
