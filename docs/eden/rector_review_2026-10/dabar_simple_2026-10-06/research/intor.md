## Shortness and structure

The InTOR public-facing model is very short: a repository landing/about page, DSpace browse/search/communities, a feedback route and item-level records. The matching re3data record supplies the structured policy summary (access, upload, licences, languages, software, identifiers and versioning) more clearly than the public policy page itself.

## Metadata versus files/licensing

- **Metadata/database:** re3data lists CC0 and Public Domain database licences. The homepage also advertises metadata export through APP.
- **Files/data:** re3data lists a general CC data licence, but item-level access is mixed (open, embargoed, institution-restricted or closed) and individual records expose rights such as Open Access, Restricted Access, Metadata Only, Embargoed or Closed. Therefore CC0/Public Domain metadata should not be read as a CC0 licence for uploaded files.

## Deposit, access, content and languages

The repository is focused on Torlak research outputs and project publications, with datasets represented as a smaller content class. The public homepage exposes communities, authors, titles, subjects, date/type filters and item records; records can include full text or restricted/metadata-only access. Re3data confirms institutional-membership restrictions for upload and for one restricted-access mode, while the repository language pair is English and Serbian. The Torlak official site provides the institutional context and links users to InTOR.

## Useful practice for UIST, with boundaries

Transferable patterns are a very short repository/about page, an explicit institutional scope, a visible feedback/support address, item-level rights/access states, and structured discovery through communities, authors, titles and subjects. UIST can use these as presentation ideas while retaining its own Macedonian/English scope, Pavel Taskov manager role and CC0 metadata rule.

Do not import InTOR’s Torlak/RCUB two-institution technical arrangement, DSpace/HDL/ORCID claims, APP/Altmetric/Scopus/Web of Science integrations, Serbian legal context, or its file-licence wording as UIST commitments. The re3data entry is a registry description; for UIST it is evidence of a compact comparator pattern, not a policy template or proof that UIST will provide those services.
