# DAIS (Serbia): policy research for a simpler UIST policy

> Comparator research retained from policy preparation. UIST use/adaptation suggestions below are historical; the [accepted pack](../README.md) governs current UIST wording and responsibilities (rector acceptance reported 6 October 2026).

Research date: 2026-10-06. DAIS is a supporting Serbian institutional-repository reference; DABAR remains the primary UIST reference.

## Sources and ownership

- Official home: <https://dais.sanu.ac.rs/?locale-attribute=en>
- Official policy: <https://dais.sanu.ac.rs/Files/policy-dais-en.html>
- Serbian policy counterpart: <https://dais.sanu.ac.rs/Files/policy-dais-sr.html>
- Official About/contact wrapper: <https://dais.sanu.ac.rs/contact>
- Matching registry record: <https://www.re3data.org/repository/r3d100013425>

DAIS’s official policy describes a joint repository of the Serbian Academy of Sciences and Arts (SASA) and research institutes under SASA. RCUB is named as the DSpace platform’s technical developer/provider. The stated purpose is open access to SASA outputs plus long-term preservation. The official contact is `dais@sanu.ac.rs`.

The re3data record lists DAIS as an **institutional** repository with nine institutions, started in 2017, and identifies RCUB as data/service provider. Its note that DAIS was CoreTrustSeal certified until 2025-09-15 is historical registry information, not a current certification claim.

## Policy size and structure

The English policy is one public HTML page of about 2,114 words with 21 labeled headings. It covers designated users; submission and registration; content scope, types and formats; access; identifiers; preservation and versioning; legal/harvesting rules; privacy; terms of service; and helpdesk. A shorter UIST policy can retain scope, deposit, access/licensing and preservation, while linking UIST’s existing privacy PDF.

## Content and languages

The official policy accepts SASA/institute publications (including historical and digitised items), member/staff publications, research data and other project outputs. Its 11 explicit types are publications/preprints, dissertations, master’s and undergraduate theses, working/technical papers, white papers/reports, research data, conference presentations, conference posters, course/training materials, images, and audio/video. The list is non-exhaustive; objects must be completed digital items. Preferred open preservation formats are advised.

The registry lists repository languages **Serbian** and **English**, and broad content classes **dataset, image, text**. Those registry labels do not replace the richer official content list.

## Deposit, access and preservation

DAIS deposit requires SASA or institute affiliation, institutional-email registration, valid credentials and repository-manager approval. The policy distinguishes internal users, project associates and the general public; only the first group deposits. Public users can browse metadata and download open content.

Metadata are freely accessible/reusable under **CC0**, including for harvesting. Documents are normally public but may be embargoed or restricted by rights/contracts; each item has its own licence, and access requests are decided by the content owner. Depositors grant SASA a non-exclusive dissemination/preservation right and must clear copyright, privacy and funder/collaborator issues.

Handles are normally assigned unless a DOI exists; ORCID is supported. Metadata and files are stored permanently, with exceptional removal only. Metadata may be corrected; substantive file updates become linked versions. The registry reports open/embargoed/restricted data access, institutional-membership upload restriction, CC/CC0 data licences, Handle, ORCID, DSpace, versioning and Dublin Core.

## UIST adaptation

Use Pavel Taskov/rectorate-appointed management as the approval point and make deposit eligibility explicitly **UIST institutional depositors only**. External collaborators and external partners must be excluded from UIST deposit eligibility; do not import DAIS’s associate/project-user model. Keep Macedonian/English versions aligned, use CC0 for metadata, assign file-level licences, allow open/embargoed/restricted access, and state permanent preservation, exceptional removal and linked versioning. Treat re3data’s broad classifications and historical certification note as registry facts, separate from the policy.
